## 1. Executive summary

1. **Where Classic stands.** The current brand site is the most honest and best-engineered site in the set, and one of the least experiential. It ships 2.2 KB of gzipped JavaScript and no third parties, its gold rate is correct without JavaScript, every piece has a prefilled WhatsApp message and the structured data is clean (file 02). It also gives images 14 % of the home page, sets 12 of 14 sections on the same ivory, opens 12 of them with the same eyebrow-and-40-px-headline template, animates nothing and spends 1,825 words explaining. It scores 4 out of 10 on experience in §3. The UAE regional median is also 4; the global luxury houses score 7 to 9.
2. **The local bar is low.** Almost every UAE Indian-heritage chain and Gulf brand reviewed interrupts within five seconds (app, voucher, newsletter or spin-the-wheel modals), bakes headline copy into banner images, or rotates carousels faster than anyone can read them (Malabar's promise carousel every 2.5 s; Damas and Bhima heroes every 3 s). Kalyan's UAE home is broken and Sky Jewellery has no website at all (files 03 to 05). Mahallati is the only regional site that feels like a house. A calm, specific, fast site would already outclass the souq online.
3. **Nobody owns the gold rate, and it is Classic's biggest opening.** Of 25 sites only four publish a rate: Malabar in a hover drop-down at four decimals with two conflicting figures, Joyalukkas on a designed page with a date but no time and no 21K, Kalyan behind three drop-downs and a Submit button, Bhima with no date or time (files 03, 04). No Gulf brand and no global house shows one (files 05, 06). None shows 21K by default, none shows a trend, and only Joyalukkas leads from the rate to a visit. Classic's feed already matches the Dubai retail rate to the fils (22K AED 465.25 on 13 October, identical to Joyalukkas and Malabar's retail table) and keeps its history in git.
4. **Enquiry-led is how luxury sells, not a fallback.** Van Cleef & Arpels makes "Order by phone" the filled button and the cart the outlined one. Cartier and Bvlgari carry the piece reference into their booking forms. Graff runs an enquire, appointment and phone band under every product, and Tamannaah sets Enquire beside Add to cart (files 06, 07). The two regional enquiry-led sites end in dead ends: Joyalukkas's lookbook pieces have no names or links and Bhima's piece page has no action at all. Classic already has the right tools (prefilled WhatsApp per piece, Reserve to view at a named branch). They need to lead the page, not trail it.
5. **The branches are a battleground.** Kalyan lists stores at Gold Centre Deira, Rolla Square, Safari Mall Muweilah and Madinat Zayed Extension, next to all four open Classic branches. Joyalukkas trades in the Gold Souq and Rolla, Damas has three units in Madinat Zayed Shopping Centre, and Pure Gold and L'azurde both sit in the Gold Souq (files 03 to 05). Their branch information is text-only, hidden behind form fields, or a map without hours. Four rich branch pages with photos, floor and parking, confirmed hours and an open-now line would win this locally. Build the module for up to seven branches: `site.ts` already lists two more Abu Dhabi locations as opening soon.
6. **The gap is photography, not code.** Every product photo is 600 × 600 on dark velvet and is sharp up to about 300 CSS px on a phone. Only four files can run edge to edge, and only on 1× screens. The current hero is a stock-looking still life of rose-gold rings on blue paper (file 02). Until new photography exists, the redesign has to get its drama from colour fields, type scale, layout, motion and the live rate. The owner's single highest-leverage action is a one-day shoot (§6).
7. **The velvet photos want a dark stage: not white tiles, and not plum.** Tiffany's category tiles, Pure Gold's category row, Joyalukkas's lookbooks and Van Cleef's films all show that gold on a dark ground reads as luxury. File 08 prototyped the fix: a near-black indigo stage (`#0b0918`) sampled from the velvet, with each photo's edges feathered by `mask-image` so the 600 px frame disappears. The same photo on plum leaves a visible navy box, so velvet shots never sit directly on plum.
8. **Pacing is the cure for "blog".** The luxury houses give 45 to 67 % of the home page to one image or film at a time and separate the moments with tall, nearly empty bands (Tiffany: 356 to 464 px holding one heading). Tanishq changes background every one or two sections. Classic should run a stage, ivory and plum rhythm, never repeat a module type back to back, and cut home-page copy by more than half.
9. **Motion is affordable and safe.** Everything the redesign needs can be built in CSS under the current CSP, with about 5 KB of new minified JavaScript in total. That covers native cross-document view transitions (0 B, about 88 % of browsers), scroll-driven reveals behind `@supports` (about 87 %), popovers with `@starting-style`, image fades and underline wipes at the 300 to 600 ms luxury pace (files 06, 08). Astro's ClientRouter (16.2 KB) and animation libraries (blocked by the CSP) are out. Firefox and older iPhones get the finished static page.
10. **One family with the store, and one set of facts.** Today the two sites share only the plum button. They disagree on the display serif, the text face, the gold (`#BDA966` against `#BE8658`), the neutrals, the review count (782 against 680) and the warranty (lifetime against one year). Settle these before either site launches (§5). Settle the heritage line too: `site.ts` says the group has been "in the gold trade for over 35 years, more than 16 of them in the UAE" (`TODO(owner)`: the old site said 30), and the jewellery brand dates from 2024. The site must not say "35 years in Dubai".

## 2. Why the current site reads as a blog

### 2.1 The root cause is the brief

The owner is right, and the cause is not a bug. The site does exactly what `docs/design-spec.md` asked of it. The spec's thesis puts hierarchy in "1 px rules" and numbered lists, "never" in "boxes, shadows, washes or motion", and rejects "mood-board luxury" (`design-spec.md:55`). It then bans alternating section backgrounds, text on images, image hover, scroll reveals, video, carousels and page transitions, and fixes every layout at a 5/7 split inside one 1,280 px column. That is a sound brief for a reference document, and it produced one. The redesign starts by amending the spec (§8.0), not by restyling components.

### 2.3 Ranked causes

1. **Images are small, framed and secondary.**
   - No image on any page runs edge to edge; the widest on the home page is 672 px, 47 % of a 1440 screen.
   - `--container` and `--container-wide` are both `80rem` (`tokens.css:95-98`), so nothing can widen.
   - The hero is a 4:5 crop of a 3:2 photo inside a 1 px bordered frame (`Hero.astro:180-185`, `:271-281`), discarding about 47 % of the photo's width.
   - Category tiles show the piece about 110 px tall, because each piece fills only the left 40 % of its 800 × 318 file.
   - On mobile the first image starts 701 px down an 844 px screen, so 83 % of the first screen is text.
   - _Fix:_ the dark stage (idea 1), a real wide track, a portrait-first hero (ideas 2, 10).
2. **One surface, one template, one rhythm for 13 screens.**
   - 12 of 14 sections share the ivory; 9 of 13 breaks are the same 1 px `#E6E0D6` rule (`base.css:418-421`); every section uses the same `--section-y` padding and starts at the same 80 px left edge.
   - 12 sections open with a 12 px gold eyebrow, a 40 px Cormorant H2 written as a sentence, an optional lead and an "All … →" link (`SectionHead.astro:39-58`). Twelve H2s on one page share size, weight, colour and alignment.
   - The two plum moments are a 1,123 px rate table and a two-column text band.
   - _Fix:_ band rhythm, tall one-line bands, never the same module twice in a row (ideas 4, 9).
3. **Nothing moves, by rule.**
   - 0 CSS animations; all 223 transitions are 150 ms colour or border changes (`tokens.css:111-115`).
   - The spec bans image hover, scroll reveals, parallax, carousels, video and page transitions (`design-spec.md:28`, `:452-453`).
   - The live rate is swapped in place "without animation" (`design-spec.md:319`), so the one number on the site that really changes never looks alive.
   - _Fix:_ a quiet motion layer (ideas 6, 11, 12) and a rate that visibly updates only when the value changes (idea 2).
4. **The page explains instead of showing.**
   - 1,825 words on the home page, about 130 per screen, peaking at 251 (FAQ) and 241 (promises).
   - The six-row promises list takes 793 px; the twelve-row collections index takes 1,110 px with no images, although every collection has a logo and a photo; four store rows have no photo or map; the rate board spends 1,123 px on five numbers and two footnotes.
   - _Fix:_ show the collection, the piece and the branch; move explanations to the pages where buyers look for them.
5. **Literal blog furniture.**
   - A journal index on the home page: three cards with a cover, "13 October 2026 · 5 min read", a two-line title and a clipped excerpt. All four posts carry the same `pubDate`, so the dates look auto-generated.
   - `/gold-rate/` has a sticky "On this page" contents list beside long prose; `/about/` is a 68-character column with pull quotes in the margin.
   - About 15 of the 22 `<Hero>` uses pass no image, so `/jewellery/`, `/collections/`, `/stores/`, `/gold-rate/` and others open like article headers.
   - Breadcrumbs above every H1, an eyebrow above every H2 and FAQ accordions at the foot of every page type.
   - _Fix:_ journal off the home page, a visual opener on every index, FAQs only on the rate, exchange and piece pages.
6. **The type is bookish and flat.**
   - Headings use Cormorant Garamond 500 (`tokens.css:58`), which the spec rules out by name (§11 line 524) in favour of Bodoni Moda. At 500 its thin strokes and small lowercase read as book type.
   - The H1 is 3.96× the body size and every H2 is 40 px. There is no display moment above 72 px, no italic and no tracking contrast.
   - Every headline is a sentence ending in a full stop ("Six promises, with their conditions."), which is how article titles look.
   - _Fix:_ one display moment per screen, a deliberate display serif in light weights with true italics, headlines that name things (idea 4, §5.2).
7. **Three unrelated photo styles, two of them stock-looking.**
   - Velvet product busts (corners `#0E0D1A` to `#44385E`); pastel still lifes on teal paper, a mint box, peach plaster and a pink-violet shell; and lilac studio cut-outs (`#E9E5E9`). None of the lifestyle images uses plum or bronze.
   - The hero file is named `story/store-lifestyle.jpg` but shows two rose-gold rings on blue paper. A stock-looking still life as the hero is the strongest single "blog cover image" signal on the site.
   - _Fix:_ one photographic world (the velvet, the counter, the branches) and the stock retired as the shoot arrives (§6).
8. **The velvet photos fight the white tiles built for them.**
   - The tray and `Plate` system assumed products shot on white ("photo white equals cell white", `design-spec.md:208`). The real photos are dark velvet, so they sit in white cells with hairline outlines on an ivory page, like thumbnails pasted into a table.
   - Tiles collapse before their photos load (`Plate.astro:66-67`): 6 of 8 home tiles measured 0 × 0 before scrolling, rows shift when photos arrive and fast scrolls show text-only cards.
   - _Fix:_ the dark stage, and a plate whose size does not depend on its image (§8.0).
9. **A thin catalogue, shown with complete honesty.**
   - 16 products, 9 of them necklaces; all 8 featured pieces on the home page are necklaces.
   - "1 piece" is printed seven times on the home page and on `/jewellery/`.
   - `weightGrams` is set on 0 of 16 products, so "by the gram" never has a gram on screen; every product has a single photo.
   - _Fix:_ curate by style rather than by count, show counts only once a style has several pieces, and get weights from the owner.
10. **The hero is a keyword sentence, not a moment.**
    - The H1 is a list of place names, followed by a 30-word intro, three buttons and a text link (`index.astro:92-112`).
    - The flagship variant splits 50/50, adds a blurred white badge with a hard-coded `rgba()` and a `box-shadow` (`Hero.astro:221-222`, `:273`, `:316-318`). It reads as a label stuck on, and it breaks four house rules.
    - _Fix:_ keep a short factual H1 for search, and let the rate and one piece carry the visual weight (idea 2).
11. **The navigation looks like documentation.**
    - Seven 17 px text links; a mega menu of four text columns and a small rate table, with no images (`SiteHeader.astro:83-142`); main controls are underlined "→" links and outlined rectangles.
    - _Fix:_ style previews and a "Visit us" column in the mega panel (ideas 5, 9, 14).

### 5.3 What the brand site should borrow from the store (ranked)

1. **The Personalisation flow.** A deep plum band with underline-only fields, contact-method chips (WhatsApp preselected), an "I'd like some guidance" option and a photo of a customer at the counter. Use it on custom jewellery, old-gold exchange and every branch page, with a branch selector and the piece or collection passed in.
2. **The shoppable 9:16 video modal.** It matches how Gulf and South Asian buyers already find gold on Instagram. On the brand site the side panel lists the piece, karat, approximate weight and the branch that holds it, with WhatsApp and visit actions and no price (idea 19).
3. **The occasion taxonomy and the art-directed mobile image.** Six named occasions with line icons, and a vertical crop planned for phones. Add Wedding and Newborn, and use Classic's own pieces, not a 3D render of red gift boxes.
4. **The four-step "how it works" strip.** One component for custom jewellery, old-gold exchange and diamonds.
5. **The mobile category icon row under the hero.** As a scrollable row of circular style crops in plum line or velvet photography.
6. **Budget gift tiles**, only with owner-approved ranges, as flat plum labels under real photos.
7. **The occasion calendar**, rebuilt without an account: an `.ics` download or an opt-in WhatsApp reminder, placed beside the rate.
8. **The store card skeleton** (3:2 image, name, address, a "Today ·" line, call button), filled with real data.

### 5.4 Where the brand site must do better than the store

- **Identity in the first screen.** No stock model. A Classic piece on its velvet, the live rate and two actions say "gold house in Dubai" in under a second.
- **Image honesty.** Never draw a photo wider than its source; full-bleed moments come from branch interiors, the souq, hands at the counter or macro crops that can support them.
- **Specific copy.** Karat, weight, the style's origin, the branch that holds the piece, what old gold is worth today. No "timeless".
- **Verifiable trust.** The dated Google rating from `site.ts`, real promises, the hallmark explained in words, the group's lineage as approved, four real branches. No invented reviewers.
- **A real store locator.** Photos, maps by link, floor and parking, hours only when confirmed, a WhatsApp per branch.
- **Narrative motion.** Rate numerals that update, staged entry of pieces, transitions between pages. No infinite shine loops, and everything off under reduced motion.
- **Accessibility and SEO.** One H1, titles and descriptions, JSON-LD, AA contrast (the store's white-on-lime badge is about 1.4:1).

### 5.5 How the two sites join

- The brand site stays enquiry-first. Once the store launches, a piece page on the brand site can carry a quiet "Also available to order online" link where the same piece code exists.
- The store's piece pages should carry the brand site's strengths: today's rate for the piece's karat, "See it at a branch" and a WhatsApp action prefilled with the code.
- Both headers carry the same rate chip, from the same feed.

### 6.4 Shooting rules (so the photos work with the stage)

- Use the same velvet for a whole series and note which one; the build samples its edge colour for the stage token.
- Leave at least 15 % clear velvet on every edge. The feather mask fades 14 % horizontally and 12 % vertically, so nothing of the piece or the bust may sit inside that margin. The bust base must not touch the frame edge (p01 does).
- One key light, the same position for every piece; no props in the product series. A separate lookbook series with props (diyas, petals) is optional.
- Keep RAW and full-resolution originals; export sRGB masters.
- Video: tripod, 4K capture, no music, a first frame that works as a poster.
- Signed releases for anyone recognisable; branch photos can avoid faces.
- No AI-generated pieces, no AI upscaling, no retouching that changes the metalwork.

### 6.5 Data the design needs as much as photos

- Weights for every piece (`weightGrams` is set on 0 of 16).
- Confirmed hours, shop numbers, phones, floor or level, parking and nearest entrance for Safari Mall, Rolla Mall and Madinat Zayed (`TODO(owner)` in `site.ts`), and confirmation of the Deira hours.
- The WhatsApp number (the Google listing and the old website disagree).
- Languages spoken at each branch, for the visit form.
- Which pieces are held at which branch, if the owner wants "see it at {branch}".
- The heritage figure, the promise terms and whether any budget ranges may be shown.

## 7. Experience principles

### 7.1 Gold is live

The rate is the heartbeat of the site and the one thing every visitor checks.

- **Techniques:** the stage hero with today's 22K per gram as its largest element and 24K, 21K and 18K beneath; "Updated 09:20" and the source named; numerals in tabular figures with a fixed width so a refresh never shifts layout; digits that roll for 600 ms only when a refreshed value differs from the stored one; a header chip on every page; today's rate for the piece's karat on every piece page; a rate page with karat tabs, a sparkline from the snapshot history and the change since yesterday.
- **Evidence:** only four of 25 sites publish a rate, none well (files 03, 04); Malabar and Bhima put the rate first in the nav because that is why visitors arrive; Joyalukkas is the only site that leads from the rate to its stores; Mejuri proves figures make a stronger headline than adjectives (file 07).

### 7.2 A vitrine, not a catalogue

Show each piece small, lit and alone on a dark ground, like a case in the shop.

- **Techniques:** `--stage-0 #0b0918` sampled from the velvet; edge feather via `mask-image`; photos capped at their source width; tiles of 280 to 420 CSS px; hairline cells (Boucheron) or a salon hang (Juri) with generous dark space; names and karat in champagne below; never on plum; never zoomed.
- **Evidence:** Tiffany's dark-fabric tiles at 332 px, Pure Gold's warm dark category row and Joyalukkas's maroon velvet lookbook are the most premium-looking product moments in their sets (files 04 to 06); Graff shows 400 px sources at 342 px and Cartier cards are 313 px, so small sources are normal; file 08 proved the feather removes the frame.

### 7.3 Pace like a house

One idea per screen, and the ground changes as you scroll.

- **Techniques:** a stage, ivory and plum rhythm; ivory bands 40 to 50vh tall holding an eyebrow and one serif line; never the same module type twice in a row; one oversized type moment per screen; home copy cut from 1,825 to under 800 words; centred display moments allowed; a wide track for rails and banners.
- **Evidence:** Cartier gives about 65 % of its home page to full-bleed media, Van Cleef 67 %, Tiffany separates moments with 356 to 464 px bands holding one heading, Tanishq changes colour band every one or two sections, Eden Garden alternates forest green and cream across 11 sections (files 04, 06, 07); Classic sets 12 of 14 sections on one ivory (file 02).

### 7.4 Every path ends at a person

The site has no cart, so asking and visiting must be the confident, primary actions.

- **Techniques:** on every piece, a filled "Ask about this piece on WhatsApp" with the name, code, URL and branch prefilled; an outlined "Call {branch}"; three quiet rows (book a visit with `?piece=`, ask us to call you, which branch has it); a four-step visit form; a shortlist sent as one WhatsApp message; a closing enquiry band on every collection, style and occasion page; the mobile bottom bar.
- **Evidence:** Van Cleef's phone-first button, Cartier's four service links, Graff's three-column band, Tamannaah's Enquire beside the cart, Bvlgari's booking with the SKU (files 06, 07); Joyalukkas's unnamed lookbook and Bhima's action-less piece page show the cost of getting it wrong (files 03, 04).

### 7.5 Four doors you can walk through

The branches are destinations, not footer rows.

- **Techniques:** branch pages with a shopfront photo, floor or level, parking, nearest metro or entrance, hours, an open-now line computed only from confirmed hours, directions by link, `tel:` and a branch WhatsApp; branch cards as the last module on the home page; a "Visit us" column in the mega panel; emirate labels; a schematic route that draws once; the flagship marked.
- **Evidence:** Pure Gold's photo, landmark and map per store, Mahallati's floor and parking, Cartier's and Bvlgari's live status, Graff's boutiques in the menu, Bvlgari and Van Cleef ending their home pages on a visit (files 05, 06); Kalyan sits next to all four Classic branches with a text-only list (file 03).

### 7.6 Heritage as proof, not prose

Say what the group does, show it happening, and let one line carry the history.

- **Techniques:** a small "A Classic Group house" lockup in the hero corner, footer and rate page; one ivory band with the approved heritage line from `site.ts`; the "from bar to bangle" assembled story; four-step strips that show the exchange process ("tested in front of you"); a karat guide (24K 99.9 %, 22K 91.6 %, 21K 87.5 %, 18K 75 %) beside each karat's rate; coins and bars as designed objects; every figure read from `site.ts`.
- **Evidence:** Tanishq's Tata badge, Bhima's 100-year seal and Jos Alukkas's cinematic group story show how a parent brand transfers trust (file 04); Eden Garden's leaf, sketch and ring sequence turns making into something you watch (file 07); Damas's broken placeholder heritage page and Pure Gold's About page listing group companies show what not to do (file 05).

### 7.7 Motion that arrives, never performs

Movement says the site is alive and made with care; it never moves the layout or asks for attention.

- **Techniques:** cross-document view transitions with the header, rate strip and action bar held still and a tapped piece growing into its page; images fading in over 500 ms on an ease-out curve as they decode; 1 px bronze underlines that wipe over 500 to 600 ms; section heads and cards rising 24 px as they enter, through `animation-timeline: view()`; the hero piece receding to 0.94 as you leave it; one gold hairline sheen, once; durations of 320 ms for interface and 600 ms for decoration; everything inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; nothing advances on its own.
- **Evidence:** Van Cleef's 0.5 s ease-out-expo image fades, Tiffany's 0.6 s underline wipe and Cartier's staged mega panel (file 06); Eden Garden's zero-JavaScript collage (file 07); the restless carousels of Malabar (2.5 s), Damas (3 s) and Bhima (3 s), and L'azurde's content hidden until an observer fires (files 03 to 05); browser support and budgets in file 08.

### 7.8 Made for a UAE phone first

Most visitors arrive on a phone, many on an iPhone, some reading Arabic.

- **Techniques:** portrait art direction for every hero; total fixed chrome of 120 px or less, including a 56 px bottom bar (WhatsApp, Call, Branches, Rate) with the safe-area inset; a row of style circles under the hero; snap rails that peek at the next card with a thin bronze progress line; tap and focus equivalents for every hover; Arabic home, rate and branch pages with `lang="ar-AE"`, `dir="rtl"` and IBM Plex Sans Arabic; the dirham sign as the real U+20C3 character with "AED" available to screen readers.
- **Evidence:** Joyalukkas spends 225 px and Kalyan 219 px of an 844 px screen on fixed chrome, and L'azurde 269 px (files 03, 05); Messika's bottom bar puts contact in the thumb zone (file 07); L'azurde is the only site with Arabic done properly; Damas's stories-style circle row (file 05); about one iPhone in four is still on iOS 18 or older, so every effect must be optional (file 08).

## 9. Things to avoid

### 9.1 Anti-patterns seen in competitors

**Interruptions**

- Modals on arrival: Joyalukkas's app modal at about 2 s, Damas's voucher modal that locks scrolling, Kalyan's spin-the-wheel that returns on scroll, Tanishq's newsletter modal and returning-visitor sheet, Messika's and Missoma's three layers before the page.
- Stacked bars above the logo: L'azurde's three bars (269 px on mobile); more than 120 px of fixed chrome on a phone (Joyalukkas 225 px, Kalyan 219 px).
- Several floating bubbles at once: chat, WhatsApp and back-to-top together (Joyalukkas, Tanishq).

**Heroes and media**

- Headline copy baked into banner images, often with empty alt text (Malabar, Joyalukkas, Tanishq, Bhima, Damas, L'azurde).
- Carousels faster than reading: Malabar's 2.5 s promise carousel that ignores interaction, Damas and Bhima at 3 s, Graff's hero that changes before it can be read.
- Heavy video: Pure Gold's 45.9 MB with no poster, Tamannaah's 17 MB sent to phones, Missoma's 8.6 MB, Messika's 960 × 540 file stretched to full screen; Mahallati's 4 MB with no poster.
- Upscaled rasters: Mahallati's 300 px product images at 783 px, the store build's 1.3 to 1.9× heroes, 2 to 3 MB PNG banners at Damas.

**The rate**

- Four decimals (Malabar); two different "today's rates" on one site (Malabar).
- Hover-only access (Malabar) or behind a form (Kalyan's three selects; Malabar's rate page defaulting to India and Kerala).
- No time, no date, no 21K (Joyalukkas, Bhima), or a stock photo of coins and a seedling beside it (Bhima).

**Stores and contact**

- Store lists hidden behind form fields (Malabar), defaulting to another country (Kalyan), or with no hours, photos or directions (Bhima, Kalyan).
- Foreign phone numbers on a UAE site (Malabar's +91, Kalyan's Indian toll-free number, Boucheron's UK number and CET hours).
- Untappable or garbled phone numbers (Mahallati, Damas); typos in store names (Mahallati).
- A booking iframe that looks like someone else's site (Graff).

**Products**

- Pieces named by SKU, or SKU instead of a name (Mahallati, Malabar).
- Lookbooks with no names, links or alt text (Joyalukkas).
- Names and prices only on hover, so touch users never see them (Pure Gold).
- Discount badges on every card and red sale items in the nav (Damas, Pure Gold, Tanishq).
- A piece page with no next step (Bhima); the same name repeated ten times (Bhima).
- Catalogue counts as boasts ("Gold Jewellery (6509)", Malabar).

**Motion**

- Sections that start invisible and rely on JavaScript to appear (L'azurde).
- Infinite shine and pulse loops with no reduced-motion handling (store build).
- WebGL worlds, numeric loaders and magnetic cursors (365 A Year of Cartier, Van Cleef's Egypt map): beautiful, heavy, and outside this budget and CSP.

**Content and facts**

- Corporate framing on a customer site: Investors in the main nav, chairman's messages, two-year-old press releases (Kalyan).
- Stale campaign microsites (Malabar's 2023 bridal edition) and placeholder heritage copy repeated four times (Damas).
- Cross-promoting unrelated group businesses in the header (Pure Gold's property strip). Classic Group is heritage, not a sales link to Classic Realty.
- Inconsistent facts across directories and a lapsed hosting bill (Sky).

**Accessibility**

- 12 px body text (Malabar); 10 px grey navigation at about 2.9:1 (Juri); grey prices at about 2.6:1 (Tamannaah); inactive items at 30 % opacity (Nida Tabba); white on lime at about 1.4:1 (store build).
- A hidden H1 (Cartier), an empty H1 (Mahallati), the only H1 at the bottom of the page (Joyalukkas), a 16 px H1 (Damas, Van Cleef).
- A dirham sign drawn over a remapped letter, read aloud as "e circumflex" (Cartier).
- Hover-only information with no tap or focus equivalent (Nida Tabba, Tamannaah).

### 9.2 Template clichés

- A stock Western model in a grey top as the hero (store build), or a celebrity grin with 3D gold lettering (Joyalukkas, Malabar).
- "Timeless", "Start the discovery", "Welcome to", "Discover", "Elevate", "timeless elegance", em dashes and emoji (all banned in `CLAUDE.md`).
- Shining lime, red and green sale badges; gradient label bands; frosted-glass chips (store build).
- A hover-only "Add to cart" bar sliding up inside the image (store build).
- An imitation WhatsApp chat window with an emoji greeting (store build).
- A footer of 26 dead links and social icons pointing at bare platform domains (store build).
- 3D-rendered gift boxes and sparkle stars on a gradient (store build).
- Pastel gradient bands and stock still lifes on teal, mint and peach (Bhima; Classic's current lifestyle images).
- A trust row of generic icons with claims that do not fit the product ("Skin safe jewellery" for 22K gold, store build).
- Journal cards with identical dates and "5 min read" on the home page (current brand site).
- Text-only page openers with an eyebrow, an H1 sentence and three buttons (current brand site).

### 9.3 Classic-specific traps

- **Velvet photos on plum.** File 08's prototype showed a navy halo box. Separate them with ivory or a bronze hairline.
- **Drawing any photo wider than its source**, scaling above 1, or zooming on hover. AI upscaling is a misrepresentation of the goods.
- **Fading in the hero or the LCP element**, or applying `@starting-style` to it.
- **Counting-up numbers.** Only the real stored rate may move, and only when it changes.
- **Invented facts.** No hours, prices, stock, ratings or statistics that are not in `site.ts` and confirmed. Budget bands only with owner approval. No FAQ markup, no fabricated offers in structured data.
- **"35 years in Dubai".** The approved line is "in the gold trade for over 35 years, more than 16 of them in the UAE", pending the owner's choice between 35 and 30. The jewellery brand opened in 2024.
- **Copying percentage promises** ("100 % value", "zero deduction") from Malabar or Joyalukkas. Describe the process instead ("weighed and tested in front of you, paid at the day's rate") until the owner confirms terms.
- **Astro's ClientRouter, GSAP, Lenis, Swiper or Lottie.** The CSP blocks third-party scripts, and the router alone would exceed the JavaScript budget.
- **Autoplaying video on phones**, YouTube or Vimeo embeds, or several map iframes on one page.
- **Two golds, two serifs, two review counts** across the brand site and the store.
