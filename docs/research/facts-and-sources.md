## Findings

### (a) 916 hallmark meaning

_Confidence: high_

'916' is the millesimal fineness mark for 22-karat gold: 22/24 x 1000 = 916.67, rounded to 916, i.e. 91.6% (strictly 91.67%) pure gold with 8.33% alloy (copper/silver/zinc) added for hardness. Corresponding marks: 24K = 999 (999.9 for bullion), 22K = 916, 21K = 875, 18K = 750, 14K = 585 (one Gulf News report prints 583). '916' and '22K' stamps mean the same thing. In India the mark is administered by BIS (triangular BIS logo + 916 + HUID since 2021); in the UAE it is the fineness number stamped under the UAE/foreign hallmark regime below. Safe copy: '916 hallmarked = certified 22-karat, 91.6% pure gold'.

### (a) UAE hallmarking law and authorities

_Confidence: high_

Federal Law No. 11 of 2015 'Concerning Monitoring Trade in Precious Stones, Precious Metals and its Stamping' (issued 3 Nov 2015, gazetted 15 Nov 2015, effective 16 May 2016; abrogated Federal Law 9/1993). Art. 4: sale/offer/possession-for-sale of wrought precious-metal articles is prohibited unless stamped with the official UAE hallmark or a recognised foreign hallmark; where the piece is too small to stamp it must be accompanied by an Identification Card. Art. 6 exemptions include gold/platinum articles under 1 gram and silver under 5 grams, unwrought metal, antiques. Executive regulation: Cabinet Resolution No. 45 of 2018 (sets legal carats per Art. 2 + Annex 1). Amended by Federal Decree-Law No. 17 of 2020, which replaced 'Authority' (ESMA) with 'Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology (MoIAT)'. Penalties as reported at enactment: dealing in un-hallmarked jewellery = up to 1 yr prison and/or AED 250k-500k fine; forging hallmarks/certificates = min 2 yrs and/or AED 500k-1m (penalty figures from 2015-16 press, not re-verified against the 2020 amended text). Copy guidance: say 'every piece carries a UAE-recognised hallmark as required by Federal Law 11 of 2015'; do not quote penalty amounts.

### (a) Dubai Central Laboratory, inspections and 'Bareeq'

_Confidence: high_

In Dubai the Dubai Central Laboratory (DCL / DCLD), a department of Dubai Municipality (lab in Karama), approves hallmarks, tests purity, calibrates shop scales to milligram accuracy and makes unannounced inspections; an older DCL statement cites a minimum of three inspections per retailer per year and ~98% compliance. A hallmark certifies karat/purity, the manufacturer's mark and a certification number. 'Bareeq' is DCL's VOLUNTARY certification awarded to retailers that pass all quality-control, labelling and fair-trading checks on top of the mandatory inspections; certified stores may display it on premises and in advertising. A 'Bareeq Excellence Award' was launched by DED + Dubai Municipality in 2013. Jan 2023: Dubai Municipality signed an MoU with Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group to receive samples at a dedicated spot in the Gold Souq so customers need not go to Karama, with test reports in one working day instead of seven. 14 Oct 2025 (GITEX): DCL unveiled a self-service AI/X-ray/spectroscopy purity-testing kiosk for gold, silver and platinum giving results in under one minute, first unit to be installed at the Deira Gold Souk. Copy guidance: only claim 'Bareeq certified' if the owner confirms the store holds it; otherwise say 'inspected under Dubai Municipality's Dubai Central Laboratory regime'.

### (b) Karat purities and typical uses

_Confidence: high_

24K = 99.9%/999 (999.9 for bullion): bars, coins, investment; too soft for daily wear. 22K = 91.67%/916: the standard for Indian, Singaporean and much Middle-Eastern bridal/traditional jewellery (Kerala, Rajkot, Kolkata, Temple work are typically 22K); Gulf News 2018 describes 22K as the most popular karat in Dubai's trade. 21K = 87.5%/875: 'long-standing Gulf tradition', the most popular karat for rings, bridal sets, bracelets and chains among Arab/GCC buyers (UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Egypt, Levant) and the reference for the Gulf daily price in Arab-market retailers; also common in Kuwaiti and some Turkish work. 18K = 75%/750: the hardness standard for diamond- and gemstone-set jewellery, engagement rings, white/rose gold, and the Italian/Western-European fine-jewellery standard. 14K = 58.5%/585: Western/Turkish everyday and heavy-wear pieces (Turkish market sells mainly 14K and 22K; sources conflict, so avoid stating a single 'Turkish karat'). Dubai typically trades 18, 21, 22 and 24K. Copy guidance: '22K for our Indian collections, 21K for Gulf and Turkish pieces, 18K for diamond settings, 24K for coins and bars' is defensible; name the Turkish/Kuwait collection karats only after the owner confirms the actual stock.

### (c) Making charges concept in Dubai

_Confidence: high_

Dubai jewellery price = (gold rate/g x net weight) + making charge + 5% VAT on the subtotal (VAT is charged on gold value plus making charge, per retailer invoices and tax guidance). Making charges are quoted per gram in AED (Dubai norm) or as a percentage of gold value; Dubai Jewellery Group chairman Tawhid Abdullah (Dec 2024) cited a Dh3-Dh30 per gram range and called Dubai's labour charges 'the most competitive anywhere in the world'; other guides give 5-8% for plain Souq pieces and far more for intricate/bridal work. Making charges are negotiable; the gold rate is not. A hallmark-compliant invoice should itemise price per gram, weight, making charge and VAT. '0% making charge' promotions are typically limited to older collections and DJG officials discourage running them constantly. 'Zero deduction on old gold exchange' is a common UAE retailer promise but usually means exchange CREDIT toward a new purchase at the day's rate (fresh making charges + VAT on the new piece apply), not a cash buy-back without deduction — Gulf News notes published retailer terms distinguish exchange credit from cash buy-back and may exclude the original making charges/VAT from valuation.

### (c) VAT: 5% on jewellery vs zero-rated investment gold

_Confidence: high_

UAE VAT (Federal Decree-Law 8/2017, in force 1 Jan 2018): gold jewellery is standard-rated at 5% on the full selling price including making charges. Art. 45(8) of the Decree-Law zero-rates 'the supply or import of investment precious metals'; Art. 36 of the Executive Regulation (Cabinet Decision 52/2017) defines these as gold, silver or platinum of at least 99% purity in a form tradeable on global bullion markets — e.g. 999.9 bars and 99%+ coins. Jewellery and coins below bullion purity (e.g. 22K/916 coins) are standard-rated at 5%. B2B dealer-to-dealer supplies of gold/diamonds use a reverse-charge mechanism (Cabinet Decision 25/2018, replaced and widened by Cabinet Decision 127/2024 effective Feb 2025, requiring a written declaration before supply) — this is irrelevant to consumer copy. Press often says investment gold is 'VAT-exempt'; the correct term is zero-rated. One accounting blog (ClearTax) claims the zero rate applies only to B2B sales, which conflicts with the statute wording and with common Dubai retail practice of selling 24K bars to consumers at 0% VAT — flag for a UAE tax adviser if the site states the 0% figure. Safe copy: 'Jewellery carries 5% VAT; 24-karat bars and coins of 99%+ purity are zero-rated for VAT under UAE law.'

### (e) UAE consumer protection and how to phrase a '14-day return policy'

_Confidence: high_

Framework: Federal Law No. 15 of 2020 on Consumer Protection (replaced Federal Law 24/2006), amended by Federal Decree-Law 5/2023, with Executive Regulations in Cabinet Resolution No. 66 of 2023 in force from 14 Oct 2023. Statutory position: (1) Defective goods — supplier must repair, replace or refund free of charge; a 'no refund' sign cannot override this; Executive Regulation penalty tables cite AED 250,000 fines for failing to repair/replace/refund defective goods; if the same defect recurs three times in the first year a full refund/replacement is mandatory; repairs over 7 days require a free substitute. (2) Change of mind — NO statutory right to return non-defective goods; Dubai Economy's consumer-awareness official: 'change of mind is not considered an accepted reason for refund/exchange unless the retailer policy provisions a return or refund for such cases'. (3) Retailers must display/communicate their return, refund and exchange policy before purchase (in-store signage and on the website), and blanket invoice clauses 'sold goods are neither returnable nor exchangeable' are unlawful (DED 2018); law-firm guidance says 'no return/no exchange' terms are unenforceable except for custom-made or perishable goods. (4) Invoices, warranty and maintenance policies must be in Arabic (other languages alongside); contract terms limiting statutory liability are void. (5) Promotions need prior approval from Dubai authorities. Correct phrasing therefore: 'Our 14-day return and easy-exchange policy is Classic Jewels' own commitment for unworn pieces in original condition with invoice and certificate; customised and engraved pieces are exchange-only; it is in addition to, and never limits, your statutory rights under UAE Federal Law 15 of 2020 for any defective item.' Do NOT say '14-day return as required by UAE law'. Complaint channel: Dubai DET consumer rights 600 545 555 / consumerrights.ae; Ministry of Economy 600 522 225 (number from a 2018 article; verify).

### (f) Diamond certification bodies in Dubai and 'tested & certified'

_Confidence: high_

Labs physically operating in Dubai (all in DMCC free zone, JLT): GIA Laboratory DMCC opened 19 Feb 2024 on two floors of Uptown Tower, grading D-to-Z natural diamonds up to 3.99 ct at launch, appointment-only intake initially limited to free-zone clients (GIA closed Antwerp the same month). ### (g) PAMP Suisse bars and 22K/24K coin conventions in Dubai

_Confidence: high_

PAMP = Produits Artistiques Métaux Précieux, registered as PAMP SA in 1977 at Castel San Pietro, Ticino, Switzerland; part of MKS PAMP Group (HQ Geneva; MKS and PAMP merged 30 Nov 2021; MKS opened a Dubai office in 1996). One of the LBMA Good Delivery referee refiners; bars are 999.9 fine (above LBMA's 995 minimum); the 'Lady Fortuna' reverse design dates from 1979 (first decorated minted bar); CertiPAMP tamper-evident assay card (1990s) carries serial number and Swiss assayer signature; Veriscan app-based surface-authentication launched 2015 (verifies identity, not purity). Minted sizes run 0.3 g-1 kg plus oz and 1-5 tola; common Dubai retail sizes 1 g, 2.5 g, 5 g, 10 g, 20 g, 50 g, 100 g (2.5 g and 20 g not confirmed on the sourced page). PAMP bars are accepted for settlement on DMCC/DGCX. Coin conventions in Dubai: coins are sold in both 22K (916, alloyed for durability, VAT 5% because below 99%) and 24K (999 or 999.9, zero-rated VAT when >=99% and bullion-tradeable); 24K coins/bars avoid making charges and design premiums (Joyalukkas CEO, Aug 2026) and carry only a small premium over the gram rate (~0.5-2% cited for bars); common coin weights seen in UAE promotions include 1 g, 2 g, 4 g, 8 g, 10 g, 20 g and 1 oz but weights stocked by this store are unverified.
